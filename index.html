04 · Why machine learning

What do you actually gain from a machine-learning model here?

Wildfire risk isn't evenly spread across time or space, but the signal that predicts it — a dry spell colliding with high wind, a cluster of recent satellite detections, a cell near dense development — is buried in dozens of noisy, correlated variables that shift every day. A model's job is to learn how those variables combine to raise or lower risk directly from real historical outcomes, instead of relying on a handful of rules a person picked in advance.

🧩 Weighs dozens of signals at once

Recent fire activity at four time lags, real fire-weather variables (temperature, humidity, wind, fuel moisture, energy release), population and road density, season, and location — combined nonlinearly, for every grid cell, every day. No fixed rule set or human analyst reviews that many interacting variables consistently at national scale.

🎯 Turns a haystack into a watchlist

New ignitions are rare — well under 1% of grid-cell-days. Ranking cells by relative risk concentrates limited attention on the small slice most likely to ignite next, rather than treating every location as equally worth watching.

📊 Gives a real, tunable probability

Because the model is calibrated, its output reads as an actual probability, not just a yes/no flag — so you can choose your own tradeoff between catching more real ignitions and watching fewer places, a dial a fixed rule can't offer.

🔄 Updates daily, not once a season

Traditional seasonal outlooks are issued monthly for a whole region at once. This kind of model rescoring happens for every cell, every day, the moment new weather and satellite data lands — a rolling forecast instead of a static snapshot.

🔍 Tests its own assumptions

A coordinate-ablation test (below) measures how much predictive skill comes from location and season alone versus real fire-weather and activity signals — machine-verified evidence that weather and recent fire history carry real, independent information.

This is the same reasoning behind the address-lookup map later on this page: instead of one seasonal severity rating for an entire state, it returns a live, cell-specific, date-specific probability, recomputed the moment new weather or satellite data comes in.
