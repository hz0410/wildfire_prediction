04 · Who this helps, and how

A probability is only useful if it triggers a decision

"61% risk" means nothing on its own. It matters only alongside a specific person, a specific action, and a specific deadline. Four groups of users and the concrete decisions this kind of daily, cell-level probability is built to inform:

🛡️ Emergency management

Helps incident commanders and dispatch decide where to pre-position engines, crews, and containment resources before a red-flag day, using the ranked cell list rather than a single regional outlook. Missing a real ignition is far costlier than a wasted patrol, so this use case leans on the wide-net 80%-recall threshold.

⚡ Utilities & grid operators

Helps power-grid operators decide where to consider targeted Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS) ahead of high-wind events, weighing outage costs for residents against ignition risk from energized lines in the highest-risk cells. A tighter, 5%-alert-budget threshold limits shutoffs to where the risk-to-cost tradeoff is clearest.

💼 Insurance & risk underwriting

Enables dynamic, daily risk signals for high-exposure property portfolios — a rolling input to underwriting and concentration-risk review, instead of one static wildfire-zone rating reassessed once a year.

🏠 Residents & communities

Turns the same probability into a plain-language risk band and a prevention checklist for one address — informing a personal decision (clear brush, review an evacuation plan, watch for red-flag warnings) rather than a raw number. Try it in the lookup tool below.

These threshold choices aren't just modeling details — missing a real ignition and raising a false alarm carry very different real-world costs depending on who's using the number. See the full cost-of-error breakdown on the Methodology page.
